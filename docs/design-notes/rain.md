# Rain: Impacts Drawn from Distributions, and the Objects They Strike

Status: experimental v1 on `work/rain-20261027`, for the author's audition. Not merged. Sample 18 (*Rain on Glass*) uses it.

## Problem

The author asked whether two axes form a musical expression:

1. Texture as a distribution of sound, not bound by consonance.
2. Short resonant tones combined with that texture.

Rain is the inspiration, not the target. Reproducing rain is not the goal.

The offline prototypes in `.orchestration/units/TEX-1/` gave three findings that hold beyond rain:

- **Exact repetition sounds artificial.** In A, 28 Voices each repeated one fixed strike. The author heard the same repetition. A2 drew every strike fresh and sounded more natural. Auditory listeners learn exactly repeated sounds quickly, and they hear textures through their time-averaged statistics. A texture source therefore keeps its statistics fixed and draws every event anew. A Voice keeps a fixed body, so it cannot do this.
- **Common onset binds a tone to the texture.** In C3, objects were struck by the drops that landed on them, at the same instant and with level following the drop. The author heard it as one world. In C2, objects were struck independently and the author heard a separate layer. Both are expressions. Grouping by common onset is the auditory scene-analysis principle that separates them.
- **One material reads as drops; scattered modes read as crumpling.** In C4, strikes were band-limited noise bursts, and the author found them unnatural. The first Rust impacts (M0/M1) placed six modes at random within half an octave, decaying in 6 ms, and the author heard them as crumpling ("くしゃくしゃ"), not rain. A2 and C3, heard as natural and rain-like, struck one inharmonic pattern (wine glass) every time, with 60-cent mode offsets, decays of 25-50 ms and a soft onset. Listeners judge material from a body's mode structure and damping. When every event has a new random structure, each event is a different object, and a run of them sounds like something being crumpled. When every event shares one structure, the events group into one material struck many times. Each event then has a momentary pitch, but the pitch changes every time, lasts tens of milliseconds and is never sustained, so the texture carries no melody or harmony.

## Concept

Rain is a third composition mode, beside Populations and Flora. Each Rain has:

- a stream of impacts over a frequency extent, every impact drawn from distributions;
- optional resonant objects that belong to the Rain and ring when an impact lands on them.

The coupling contract matches Flora's. Rain, Flora and Populations affect each other only through radiated sound and the Landscape. Voices are never struck by Rain. The separate-layer expression (C2) is a Rain placed beside a Population.

## Impacts

- **Timing.** A Poisson process at the composer's `rate` (impacts per second). There is no beat unless `follow_meter` is positive (next item but one). The process runs by time rescaling: a unit-exponential budget is drawn after each impact and the current rate is subtracted per sample, so it stays Poisson while the rate changes, and live `rate()` patches take effect at once.
- **Sway.** `sway(octaves)` lets the rate fluctuate as `rate · 2^{sway · g(t)}`, where `g` has unit variance and a 1/f spectrum (the author's proposal after S1). `g` sums nine equal-variance AR(1) processes with time constants 0.4 s · 2^k, k = 0..8 (0.4 s to 102 s), which gives 1/f between about 0.0016 and 0.4 Hz. The fastest stays below the pulse range (about 0.5 Hz and up), so the sway is heard as changing intensity rather than a beat. States start from their stationary distribution, so the sway is present from placement. The swayed rate is capped at the work bound of 2000 per second. Default 0, range [0, 4]; beyond four octaves the rate spends most of its time at zero or at the bound. Struck objects follow the swayed rate, since strikes are a fraction `hit` of impacts.
- **Follow the meter.** `follow_meter(focus)` lets the impacts gather where the meter heard in the habitat expects them (the author's request on 2026-11-17: rain with a rhythmic element, filtered by temporal DCC). The Rain reads the same habitat audio-only meter that Flora reads; wiring reserves it from run start when a Rain has a positive focus at placement or in a later patch. For the beat, and for the subdivision and measure when the meter detects them, the level's confidence `c` is a phase-locking value: the resultant of heard onset phases about that level's expected position (times the meter's presence gate). It gives the von Mises concentration `κ = A1⁻¹(c)` whose resultant is `c` (the piecewise approximation in Fisher 1993), multiplied by `focus` and capped at 512. The impact rate is multiplied by `exp(Σ κ (cos φ − 1)) / Z`, where `φ` is each level's meter phase advanced sample by sample through the hop from the observation at its start, and `Z` is the mean of the numerator over one cycle of the slowest level (2048 midpoints). The mean rate is therefore kept; impacts are moved in time, not added. At focus 1 the impacts reproduce the concentration the meter heard; above 1 they sharpen it. Because the meter hears the impacts, this is a loop: impacts gathered on a beat raise its confidence, which gathers them further, while a weaker or sparser rain lets the presence gate and the 2.5 s evidence decay loosen it. Several levels together make a metrical weighting, not one concentrated phase (DCC §4.2): impacts lean toward the beat, less toward the subdivision, and more where the measure's accent falls. An unknown, stale or unconfident meter leaves the rate unweighted, and the 2000 per second cap still applies. The weight draws no random numbers, but impacts that move across a hop boundary take later draws (the sway draws once per hop from the same stream), so later levels and places differ from the unweighted Rain's. Default 0 (the meter is not read and every impact is bit-identical to before), finite and non-negative, live-patchable from the next hop. There is no upper bound: the cap of 512 per level is where the normalising grid's aliasing error stays near e⁻¹⁶ and the pulse is about 2 degrees wide, so the per-sample rate cap governs before it. The meter is a principle input with a candidate oscillator form (as for `metric()`); the weighting, its use on the rate and the focus are author rules.
- **Band position.** At each impact the centre frequency is drawn from the placement's distribution over the extent. The distribution is rebuilt each hop from the Landscape with the spawn-placement density masses (`field_density_mass`):
  - `random(lo, hi)`: log-uniform, independent of the Landscape;
  - `consonance(lo, hi)`: follows the eroded consonance density mass;
  - `dissonance`, `edge` and `gap`: follow the same masses as Voice placement.
  - When the in-range mass is zero, the distribution is log-uniform.
- **Leave drops out.** `leave_drops_out(enabled)`, initial only, default false. When true, the Rain reads a field without its own impacts: the renderer captures the impacts' actual habitat samples (scaled exactly as added to the bus), and an analysis observer built like a Voice's subtracts them before the auditory front end. The Rain's objects stay in that field, so in a Rain-only scene the field is the objects' sound. Impact positions under `consonance`, `dissonance` and `edge`, object drift and `ring` read it, with the shared habituation of the mixed habitat applied (`erode_score`, `erode_mass`). `gap` keeps reading mixed occupancy: a place is free only if the impacts left it free. Before the Rain's first render it reads the mixed field, which then holds none of its sound; a hop without its observation (a gap or analysis restart) keeps the previous distribution, `ring` table and object proposals, as Flora does. No random number is added or reordered, so impact times, levels and struck objects are those of `leave_drops_out(false)`; only positions change. Presentation-only Rain warns at placement and reads the mixed field. False is bit-identical to before. H/R as input is a principle; which sound is excluded is an author rule.
- **Material.** `impact_modes(pattern)` sets the struck body with the ModePattern vocabulary; the default is `modal_table("wine_glass")`. Its ratios are taken once at placement, at the extent's geometric centre, and at most eight are used.
- **Strike.** Mode `k` sits at `centre · ratio_k · 2^{u_k}`, where `u_k` is drawn uniformly from ±`width` for every impact. Mode `k` has level `0.85^k` with a 3 dB spread and a random phase. The first mode decays with time constant `τ = 25 ms · (3.2 kHz / f_c)^{1/2}` with a log-normal spread of 0.4, and mode `k` with `τ / ratio_k^{1/2}`. Each impact starts with a raised-cosine onset of 0.5 to 1.5 ms; a hard start adds a broadband click. Level is log-normal with a 5 dB spread, capped at +9 dB. These constants reproduce the A2 prototype (25 ms over 1.5-7 kHz and 50 ms over 0.25-1.5 kHz); they are authored, not parameters of auditory cognition.
- **Ring.** `ring(octaves)` lets impacts that land on more consonant places ring longer (the author's request after RW-2: the more consonant, the better it rings). Each hop the eroded consonance field score `s` inside the extent is normalised to `u = (s - s_min) / (s_max - s_min)`; `u` is 0 everywhere when the extent is flat, silent or not finite. Because `u` is relative within the extent, the top-ranked places lengthen by the same factor even when the field has no lasting structure. An impact reads `u` at the bin where its first mode actually sounds (after the `width` offset) and multiplies the decay of all its modes by `2^{ring · u}`, so their ratios `τ / ratio_k^{1/2}` and the material are kept. Outside the extent, or in the hop a live patch first raises it, the factor is 1. The mapping is one-sided: decays only lengthen, level is unchanged (presence is not replaced by loudness), and the pitch objective sign does not invert it; the word means "more consonant", not "preferred". It is read from the eroded side, like the rest of Rain, so habituation lowers the rank of places that have rung for a while. No random number is drawn, so at 0 every impact is bit-identical to before. Default 0, live-patchable, range [0, 4]. The bound comes from failure: the decay factor is a rotating complex coefficient, which rounds to 1 in f32 once τ exceeds about 350 s at 48 kHz; the longest base τ (20 Hz, six log-normal deviations) is about 3.5 s, so 16× stays near 56 s, while 6 octaves would reach 224 s. H/R as input is a principle; the score-to-decay mapping, its octave scale and its one-sidedness are author rules.
- **Width.** `width(octaves)` is a composer control in [0, 5], live-patchable. The default 0.05 (60 cents, as in A2) keeps every impact recognisably the same material. Wider ranges scatter the modes until the material dissolves into crackles; 0 repeats the pattern exactly. The upper bound covers the audible band from any centre.
- **Pool.** 256 preallocated impact slots. A new impact takes a free slot, or the one closest to its end. The pool bounds per-sample work; it is not a musical quantity. It was 64 until RW-4: on recorded RW-2 fields, 64 slots were cut while still sounding for a third of the impacts at 120 per second without `ring`, almost all at 240 per second (median 33 dB below the impact's start), and almost all at 120 per second with `ring(2)`. A scene that never fills 64 slots renders identically; output differs from the first moment all 64 were busy, because the impact that would have been cut keeps sounding and later slot choices and summation order can differ.

## Objects

- **Count.** `objects(n)`, from 0 to 16; the default is 3.
- **Pitch.** At placement each object takes a peak of the eroded consonance field level inside the extent, with peaks at least 1 ERB apart. Pitched sound follows consonance even when the impacts are free. Missing peaks are filled by log-uniform positions in the extent.
- **Drift.** Each hop an object proposes the best eroded consonance score within one semitone of its sounding pitch, inside the extent and at least 1 ERB from the other objects. The score subtracts the Voice movement cost (1 per octave: coefficient 0.5 over the 2 s integration window), and a move must gain more than the Voice threshold 0.1. The pitch objective sign applies as it does to Voices. The object moves to its proposal only when it is next struck, so the new pitch starts with a strike and the rate of movement follows `hit`. `anchor()` holds every object at its placement pitch; the author found the drifting version more interesting (M0/M1), so drift is the default. Mode ratios stay fixed from placement; modes that reach 0.45 fs are muted, not dropped, so a retuned bank keeps its state.
- **Body.** `object_modes(pattern)` uses the ModePattern vocabulary; the default is `modal_table("vibraphone_1")`. Mode `k` has gain `0.5^k` and T60 `object_decay · ratio^{-0.7}`. The default `object_decay` is 2 s.
- **Strikes.** Each impact lands on an object with probability `hit` (default 0.06, about 2.4 strikes per second at the default rate, matching the C3 audition). The object is chosen uniformly. The impact also sounds, so the common onset is preserved. The drive is scaled per object so the ring's onset peak matches the impact's level. Common onset is what binds the two, so their onsets are made equally loud. Longer `object_decay` still adds presence.
- **Follow drops.** `objects_follow_drops(enabled)`, initial only, default false. An object holds its pitch only while it rings. It counts as silent before its first strike and once the T60 of its longest audible mode at placement has passed since its last strike; for the default body, whose lowest ratio is 1, that is `object_decay`. A silent object struck by an impact takes the impact's centre, clamped to the extent, as its new pitch, and the new pitch starts with that strike. If the centre lies within 1 ERB of another object's sounding pitch, the object keeps its pitch and is struck as usual; it is not pushed to the nearest spaced place, which would gather objects where roughness is likely. A ringing object drifts as before, and `anchor()` wins (with a warning). No random number is added or reordered, so impact times, levels and struck objects are those without the word; only object pitches change, and through the field the impact positions. With `leave_drops_out(true)` the centre is a sample of the field without the Rain's impacts, so a new pitch appears with a probability that follows its consonance with the objects still ringing. Nobody writes the pitch. How long a pitch holds is set by the composer's `rate`, `hit`, `objects` and `object_decay`: an object stays ringing through a gap between strikes shorter than its decay D, which at a per-object strike rate λ happens with probability 1 − exp(−λD). Stronger rain therefore holds pitches longer, and weakening rain lets them change. RW-7 estimated, from RW-6's strike sequence, a median hold of 8-16 s for eight objects with `rate(60).sway(1)`, `hit(0.2)` and the default 2 s decay; 2.6 s at a 1 s decay, and 46 s at 4 s, where objects rarely fall silent. The word does not guarantee change: long decays or frequent strikes keep objects ringing. False is bit-identical to before. Author rule.

## Rendering and runtime

- Rain renders after Voices and Flora, into the buses its `send()` selects. This happens before the self-sound history reads the habitat bus, so Voices hear Rain as external sound.
- Advance, impact triggering and rendering allocate nothing in steady state, with or without `leave_drops_out` and `objects_follow_drops`.
- Placement and a Log2Space change may allocate. `leave_drops_out(true)` allocates one hop of impact capture and a score scan at placement, one recycled packet entry at its first capture and its observer at birth; publication owns its H/R, score and mass scans. The observer runs on the analysis thread like a Voice observer. Reports allocate only while enabled.
- RNG: `SmallRng` seeded from the scenario seed and the Rain id.
- Release ramps the gain to zero over `fade`. A finished Rain is removed. The run waits for Rain tails, as it does for Flora.

## Why impacts do not form consonance

An impact's pitch lasts tens of milliseconds and changes with every impact, so the harmonicity impacts leave in the Landscape is brief and scattered; they mostly add roughness. The Landscape's memory is short (density smoothing of 10 ms, hop windows of tens of milliseconds), and habituation is negative feedback that erodes sustained peaks. A run of impacts therefore does not build a consonance structure by itself (TEX-1 N0/N1: no change in octave shares or spectral flatness). Consonance comes from pitched sound with slow inertia, which is the objects' role. Impacts use Landscape distributions based on level or roughness, such as `gap`, and `width` moves them toward pitch when the composer wants it. A positive `ring` did not change this in RW-4 (one Rain alone, `ring` 1, 2 and 4 on the mixed field): the depth of the field's ripple stayed about 0.13 in every intensity group, its one-second persistence fell (0.13-0.18 without `ring`, 0.05-0.11 with it), and the remaining effect was 0.7-3.4 dB more overall level. In RW-5's analysis of the same renders, the impacts carried about nine tenths of the 150-5000 Hz energy the Rain read, and longer impacts added more of it.

## Measurements (TEX-1 M0/M1, before the impact material)

Four objects, no drone, impacts on `gap(150, 5000)`, 60 s, seeds 21-24. Over all object pairs, the mean distance to the nearest of unison, 6/5, 5/4, 4/3, 3/2, 5/3 and the octave fell from 66 cents at placement to 42 cents over the last 30 s. Uniformly random intervals give 60 cents. Two seeds reached near-just chords (seed 21 ended at 180, 359, 719 and 960 Hz, every pair within 2 cents); two moved little. These numbers describe the mechanism; they do not decide whether it is heard as music. Listening to seed 21, the author found the drifting objects (M1) more interesting than fixed ones (M0) and heard the impacts as crumpling, which led to the impact material above. On the same scene and seed, the author heard the material impacts (P1) as natural and the scattered-mode impacts (M1) as artificial rather than rain.

## Sample 18 and the S1 trace

Sample 18 places a Rain with four objects on `gap(150, 5000)` with `sway(1)`, adds an unheard field (a habitat-only harmonic drone on D), turns the field up a fourth, then releases it and thins the rain. An object-pitch trace of the same scene without sway (S1, seed 21) showed the objects approaching the new harmony within a few seconds of the turn, one landing on G4 within 2 cents, and then loosening as habituation eroded the field. Before the turn they barely approached the field. The author wanted to take S1 further.

With `sway(1)` (S2, the sample as written), the author reported a very strong cognitive effect: pleasure, and the body sways by itself, although the rain has no beat. S3 added three quiet sustained voices seeking the same field, to make the unheard field audible; the author found them unnatural. They share neither the impacts' onsets nor the rain's sway, so they are heard as a separate overlay (the C2 finding) rather than part of the rain world. The sample keeps the S2 form.

## Population sway (experimental)

After the 1/f rain, the author asked what the finding implies for Conchordal. Two alternatives were tried and rejected first:
- Multi-timescale landscape habituation as a bottom-up source. It is on `research/multiscale-habituation-20261027`, and it left the onset fluctuation index unchanged.
- An offline loudness sway. The author rejected it, because changing only the output level changes nothing the ensemble does.

Rain's sway changes the event process itself, so the Voice analog changes participation density.

- `PopulationSpec.sway(octaves)`, Initial, range [0, 4], default 0.
- Each Population that sways owns one process `g` from the same 1/f source as Rain (`src/life/sway.rs`), seeded from the scene seed and the Population id.
- At every otherwise eligible onset opportunity, in every timing mode, a Voice sounds with probability `min(1, 2^(depth · g))` on a private draw and otherwise lets the opportunity pass. This is the same gate a refused onset uses, so participation clocks resolve it as a skip.
- The composer's density is the ceiling; the sway thins it. While `g` is positive nothing changes, so a Population whose slow components stay positive for a whole short piece does not thin. Sample 16's far crowd did this with seed 21.
- Pitch, loudness and sustained notes are not changed. With depth 0 no draw is taken, so behavior is bit-identical to before.
- Status: unvalidated. The only audition compared sample 16 with and without crowd sway. The author ruled it unusable for the decision because sample 16 does not function as music. This is not a negative result. Population sway waits for Voice material that works as music; until then it stays experimental, default 0, and is not used in samples.

## Caveats and exclusions in v1

- By default Rain reads the mixed Landscape, including its own impacts, so its distribution is rebuilt from its own samples. `leave_drops_out(true)` removes the impacts only; per-object self-exclusion is not implemented.
- `ring` on the mixed field did not reinforce the field (RW-4, above). With `leave_drops_out(true)`, `ring` reads a field that holds none of the lengthened impacts, so it reflects the field without feeding it back.
- With `leave_drops_out(true)` the objects hear their own sound and each other's without the impacts' noise, and they can settle. In RW-6 (one Rain, eight objects, the RW-4 scene and seed, pitches sampled once per second), five of the eight objects stayed within 25 cents of one pitch through the middle 180 s, holding octaves and fifths; the other three made eight moves of more than 25 cents, and four of the five that had a pitch before last returned to it. The read field's ripple correlated 0.70 at 30 s against 0.84 at 1 s, so the field froze. Impacts followed the field one to three seconds later (median detection statistic 8.7-9.9 by intensity group, against -0.2-0.0 for the same Rain on the mixed field). RW-7 read the freeze from the records. Within 10 s the objects settled on one harmonic lattice of about 184 Hz (peaks at 1, 3/2, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12 and 24 times it). Lattice places without an object stood as high as those with one (0.36-0.70 against 0.32-0.91 before habituation), so the other objects alone keep every object's peak, and per-object self-exclusion would not release it. Habituation lowered the lattice without reordering it (next item), so nothing was left to move the objects. `objects_follow_drops` lets a silent object leave the lattice.
- With `objects_follow_drops(true)` added (RW-8, the same scene and seed), 59 strikes in the middle 180 s retuned a silent object (0.33 per second; 0.38 in the weakest third of seconds, 0.22 in the strongest) and 69 were blocked by the 1 ERB spacing. An object kept one occupant's pitch for a median of 14.7 s (90th percentile 49 s, longest 133 s), and every 30 s window of the middle held at least one retuning. The read field's ripple correlated 0.19 at 30 s against 0.73 at 1 s, and impacts still followed it one to three seconds later (median detection statistic 5.1 in the weakest third, 7.2 in the strongest). The objects' mean distance from simple intervals was 47 cents (RW-6 10, mixed-field control 50, random pitches 61), and 45% of retuned objects drifted within 10 s. These records describe the mechanism; they do not decide how it is heard.
- Reports: `rain_field` once per second (what the Rain read over its extent and its objects' pitches) and `rain_impact` per impact (with the struck object's pitch after the strike and whether it followed the impact or was blocked by the 1 ERB spacing; with a weighted rate also `meter_weight` and `beat_phase` in cycles), only while reports are enabled. No GUI. Beat participation only through `follow_meter`.
- Objects hear their own sound and each other's. On the mixed field the impacts' noise is what moves them: in RW-6's control (the same scene and seed on the mixed field), 30% of object-seconds had a proposal gaining more than the 0.1 threshold, and the objects made 537 moves in the middle 180 s. Habituation alone does not move them. It erodes a score multiplicatively, and in RW-6 it eroded the whole lattice by about the same share (state 0.57-0.75 at peaks with an object, 0.58-0.69 at lattice peaks without one), so a peak stays a peak. Without the impacts, 1.2% of object-seconds had such a proposal (RW-7). Two objects that reach an octave or fifth can move together as a locked pair; in RW-6 four objects on an octave series moved 13 cents together in one second.
- Rain is Experimental. The technote is not updated until the author accepts the expression.
