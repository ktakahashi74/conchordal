Conchordal Script Reference

Rhythm: Evidence and Participation

A beat is a recurrent temporal reference inferred from sound. Sharing that reference does not require every Voice to attack at the same phase.

The three presets express different timing intentions:

  • metric: explicit attraction to the shared production beat. Strong coupling favors synchronized onsets.
  • entrained: a bodily pace adjusted by remembered acoustic context and a prediction of energetic overlap with surrounding sound.
  • flow: weak acoustic participation with clustered renewal intervals around an intrinsic scale that does not follow recurrence.

For entrained and flow timing, a causal observer reads past habitat audio. Each Voice also retains the sound it rendered, so the energy attributed to itself can be excluded from a predicted competitor. The Voice compares sounding near its due time, waiting up to a participation cycle, and omitting the current event. Overlap is integrated across an estimate of the planned hold and ADSR release. Successive omissions become more costly, so overlap alone cannot prescribe permanent silence. Temporal memory changes only after an onset is emitted. For entrained timing, the cadence can follow a supported recurrence near the Voice’s intrinsic scale, while preserving its own cycle position. The observer does not prescribe a common onset phase. Hold lengths remain tied to the intrinsic period, so cadence adaptation does not stretch each sound. The overlap preview uses the same hold duration. Flow keeps its intrinsic renewal scale while still waiting or omitting events in response to surrounding sound; its executed intervals therefore remain variable. This three-band energy proxy is an engineering approximation, with musical acceptance still under evaluation.

Director-level terrain

The director shapes the rhythmic terrain, symmetric to the consonance-field operations. These operations shape the production meter used by metric timing. They do not set the bodily rate or recurrence candidates of entrained/flow participants:

  • meter_stability(value) — attractor depth in [0,1], default 0 (neutral): how readily a pulse forms and how strongly its reading favors a preferred tempo. The tempo-preference weight equals this depth. The preferred center is the center of temporal_basin, or 2 Hz in the default band. It only deepens the basin for a real periodicity; it never fabricates a beat from non-metric input. This is a composer-set cultural prior of the modeled listener.
  • temporal_basin(min_hz, max_hz) — a continuous tempo region for the emergent beat (the time-axis analogue of consonance(min, max)). It seeds the beat at the center and confines adaptation to the band. At stability 0, it adds no center preference or restoring pull. Higher stability strengthens both the preferred reading and confidence-weighted center restoring. It shapes the terrain; it does not place a beat, and it never forces a measure. The same sanitized band center also sets the intrinsic starting rate of metric() Voices, independently of stability. Without a basin, that rate remains 2 Hz.

The basin is one initial condition for the whole scene. The final declaration in the script applies to every metric Population, through both place() and create(). A declaration after a Population or after wait() still replaces that same scene value; it does not change the basin during performance.

A body’s initial tempo is distinct from a beat confirmed by the listener. Metric Voices start with independent phases. Until sound corroborates a beat, confidence and shared-beat attraction remain zero. After confirmation, the existing coupling follows the heard beat. Respawn starts from the Population’s resolved template; it does not inherit the parent’s current clock rate. Entrained/flow bodily priors and explicit pulse(rate_hz) are unaffected.

From a cold start, a regular 4 Hz input in the default band is read as a 4 Hz beat at stability 0, and as duple subdivision of a 2 Hz beat at stability 1. Depth also retains its effects on forcing and evidence presence; it is not an independent control of tempo weighting. Confidence remains zero until the first interval hypothesis is corroborated. Chance corroboration in irregular input can still produce temporary rises afterward.

Per-voice presets and modifiers

The Tier-1 presets take no rate argument: metric(), entrained(), and flow(). Configure them on a PopulationSpec before calling place() or create(). Metric timing begins at the basin center, or 2 Hz without a basin, then responds to the acoustically confirmed production beat. Entrained/flow participants begin with their own 2 Hz intrinsic prior unless intrinsic_rate(hz) is supplied. Entrained cadence can follow acoustic recurrence without changing that prior or assigning a shared onset phase. Flow keeps that intrinsic scale for its irregular renewal intervals.

Do not confuse .entrained() with brain("entrain"). The preset controls onset timing; the brain selects the voice’s articulation life and metabolism. They are independent and may be used together. See Population — A Persistent Unit of Voices.

Per-voice modifiers refine these intentions:

  • entrainment(strength) — influence in [0,1]: acoustic context for entrained/flow, beat-phase attraction for metric. Zero keeps intrinsic pacing.
  • rhythm_role("beat"|"subdivision"|"accent"|"texture") — the voice’s metrical job. accent emits a stronger onset that drives the shared meter harder, so a recurring downbeat can seed an emergent measure.
  • microtiming(amount) — a signed metric beat-phase offset in [-0.5, 0.5]. A value of 0.5 targets the half-beat. It does not assign fixed positions to entrained/flow participants.
  • measure_accent(amount) — weak onset emphasis following the detected measure, in [0,1] (default 0). The onset strength multiplier is 1 + 0.35 * amount * confidence * cos(phase). The phase origin follows observed accents, and no detected measure means no emphasis. It changes strength, not onset scheduling, and works independently of rhythm_role.
meter_stability(0.85);     // attractor depth: how readily a pulse forms
temporal_basin(1.8, 2.2);   // tempo region the emergent beat gravitates toward

let beat = harmonic()
    .metric()
    .rhythm_role("accent")  // a strong onset that drives the shared beat
    .cycles(2);

let entrained = harmonic()
    .entrained()
    .cycles(2);

let drift = harmonic()
    .flow()
    .cycles(1);

let offbeat = harmonic()
    .metric()
    .microtiming(0.5)       // a half-beat offset reads as syncopation
    .cycles(2);

place(beat, at(110.0));
place(entrained, consonance(110.0).peak().count(3));
place(drift, consonance(300.0, 1200.0).count(4));
place(offbeat, at(220.0));
wait(12.0);

Except for explicit sustain plus participation described below, calls on the same axis are last-write-wins: the last timing mode and the last duration mode determine the final behavior. cycles(n) counts the intrinsic bodily period for entrained/flow and the effective beat period for metric timing; flow retains its existing threefold hold multiplier. Modifiers are remembered and applied when their matching preset is selected, so entrainment(0.8).metric() and metric().entrainment(0.8) are equivalent. The same applies to duration_range(...).adaptive_duration() and the reverse order.

Setting the body’s intrinsic rate

intrinsic_rate(hz) sets the founder Voices’ bodily scale for entrained() or flow(). Its period is 1/hz. This is a body initial condition, not an onset-interval command or a common beat. Entrained timing adapts its participation period to heard recurrence without changing the intrinsic value. Flow uses that value for irregular renewal. Both still choose to sound, wait or omit events; different intrinsic rates do not guarantee permanently different onset rates.

Set it on a PopulationSpec before place() or create(). Either order with the participation preset has the same meaning. It cannot be changed during performance. Replacements read the Population template, with fresh initial due and performance state rather than a parent’s adapted period, plan or articulation policy.

The same bodily period sets finite note lengths (cycles, duration_range), rest(periods) and the representative hold used for overlap prediction. Changing the rate therefore changes note lengths and rests in seconds together. Flow keeps its threefold hold multiplier; ADSR release remains in seconds. Explicit sustained notes without a finite duration stay alive until release.

let slow = harmonic().sustain().entrained().intrinsic_rate(0.5)
    .seek_consonance().pitch_apply_mode("gate_snap");
let quick = harmonic().intrinsic_rate(2.0).sustain().flow()
    .seek_consonance().pitch_apply_mode("gate_snap");
let a = place(slow, line(220.0, 220.0).count(1));
let b = place(quick, line(330.0, 330.0).count(1));
wait(6.0);
release(a);
release(b);

These rates are example inputs, not recommended values. A final entrained/flow specification accepts finite values in 0.01..=200 Hz. Values outside that range or nonfinite values are script errors before placement. The range protects execution; it is not a musically recommended range. Flow’s renewal interval has separate existing limits of 0.035..=4 seconds, so extreme rates do not imply proportionally scaled intervals. Other final timing forms, including metric, pulse, repeat and once, warn and ignore this modifier even if its value is invalid. Omission keeps the existing 2 Hz path, and metric’s basin rule is unchanged.

rhythm_freq(hz) sets the Entrain articulation core oscillator and does not set this participation scale. proposal_interval(seconds) controls pitch proposals; it does not command an onset or change the body’s intrinsic rate.

Sustained sound with participation opportunities

Explicit sustain() plus entrained() or flow() keeps a Tone sounding while its Voice continues to consider participation opportunities. Either builder order has the same meaning: sustain().entrained() and entrained().sustain() are equivalent. At an opportunity, choosing to sound releases the old Tone and attacks a new Tone at the body’s pitch at that sample. Waiting or omitting an opportunity retains the ongoing Tone, so omission does not create a gap. An unchanged pitch can be re-attacked.

let p = place(
    harmonic().sustain().entrained().seek_consonance().glide(0.03),
    consonance(220.0, 660.0).count(1)
);
wait(3.0);
release(p);

With the default "glide" mode, a newly adopted pitch target also offers its sample as a candidate for an undecided, legal participation opportunity. The existing timing, acoustic-context and overlap costs choose among this candidate and the existing candidates; ties retain the existing candidate, and a committed plan is not moved. If the adoption is not selected for an attack, movement glides. An adoption and clock onset at the same sample produce one attack. A clock onset during a glide uses the current body pitch; it does not jump ahead to the unfinished target. Release, death and removal cancel pending requests. Emitted attacks enter the usual energy and acoustic paths once.

This uses the existing finite representative footprint for participation prediction; it does not compare an exact rendering of continuing against release plus a new attack. It does not add a phrase-boundary detector or a pitch-interval threshold.

For this combination the omitted pitch application mode resolves to "glide". Explicit "gate_snap" with free pitch instead holds one adopted target until the Voice chooses to sound at a participation opportunity. The old Tone then releases and a new Tone attacks at that target. A later adoption replaces the pending target. Waiting and omission retain the sounding pitch and the pending target; movement does not glide. With tie omitted or zero, an opportunity without a pending target re-attacks the same pitch. GateSnap offers no extra candidate at the adoption sample and uses the unchanged participation costs and skip rule. At the same sample, adoption precedes the opportunity; a future adoption cannot affect an earlier onset. Release, death and removal cancel the pending target.

let p = place(
    harmonic().sustain().flow().seek_consonance().pitch_apply_mode("gate_snap"),
    consonance(220.0, 660.0).count(1)
);
wait(3.0);
p.pitch_apply_mode("glide");
wait(3.0);
release(p);

Live Glide ↔ GateSnap ↔ Rearticulate switching preserves the sounding Tone and emits no extra attack. It cancels unrendered requests and the pending attack target. On returning to Glide, movement follows the controller’s retained target at the next control update. GateSnap and Rearticulate wait for subsequent adoptions. Calling the same mode again keeps its pending state. Replacement Voices inherit the mode, starting with no pending performance state. Timing presets remain initial-only.

With pitch_apply_mode("rearticulate"), rearticulate_above(cents) combines sliding and deferred jumps. A small adoption cancels old pending pitch and starts gliding; a large one stops sliding and defers the target to the next sounded opportunity. With tie omitted or zero, an opportunity without a target attacks at current body pitch. Waiting and skipping do not stop existing glides. The boundary uses the last actual onset pitch, updated by every attack, and does not suppress clock attacks. Zero or omission matches GateSnap. Unlike Hold without participation, Rearticulate here attacks at a sounded opportunity rather than at adoption. Boundary changes preserve the selected behavior until the next adoption. Mode changes clear unrendered requests, pending pitch and the selected glide without changing the Tone or the controller’s target. Builder order does not change this form; replacements inherit controls, not pending pitch, selected glide or last-onset memory.

The implicit WhileAlive default and while_alive() alone do not select this form. sustain() alone, entrained() alone and flow() alone retain their previous behavior. With metric(), pulse() or repeat(), sustain() still uses the existing last-write-wins rule. Without rest(), a later finite duration via cycles() or adaptive_duration() replaces the sustained form. With rest(), finite note length and sustained participation are retained independently, and each note releases before a pause measured from actual tail completion. See Cutting notes and leaving a rest.

Retaining an articulation policy

Within free-pitch sustained participation, articulation_persistence(p) lets a Voice keep a private articulation policy across several pitch adoptions and participation opportunities. It requires sustain() plus entrained() or flow(), pitch_apply_mode("rearticulate"), and a positive rearticulate_above(cents). The value is set before placement; it is not a live Population method.

let p = place(
    harmonic().sustain().entrained().seek_consonance()
        .pitch_apply_mode("rearticulate").rearticulate_above(50)
        .articulation_persistence(0.75),
    consonance(220.0, 660.0).count(1)
);
wait(3.0);
release(p);

The boundary and persistence in this example are comparison inputs, not recommended values. The private policy starts in Base, using the existing boundary and participation rules. Connect slides adopted targets of either size while a Tone lives, and intentionally withholds opportunities that the participation policy selected to sound. It extends the current Tone rather than recording a cost skip. It creates no new Tone, onset recharge, or onset-derived context learning.

Small adoptions and cost skips propose Connect; large adoptions and newly selected sounded opportunities propose Base. For 0 < p < 1, a proposal against the current policy keeps it with probability p and changes it with probability 1-p. Matching proposals and waiting hops do not draw again. A future planned opportunity keeps its decision until execution. Entering Connect cancels pending pitch; the last actual onset remains the reference, and cancelled adoptions never replay.

Persistence is policy inertia, not a legato proportion. It acts on Base as well as Connect. Raising p need not increase Connect time or decrease the number of attacks monotonically. Duration follows existing events, not a fixed number of seconds or beats. These retained intervals are not tied to audible cadence, contour, or rests, and do not infer phrase boundaries.

The default 0 and endpoint 1 both use the existing path without a private policy or extra random draws; at 1, birth in Base never changes. Finite inputs clamp to [0,1]; positive infinity becomes 1, negative infinity and NaN become 0. An unsupported final Voice specification warns and ignores the modifier, regardless of builder order. Replacements inherit the template value and start in Base with fresh random and performance state. A live mode change resets Base; repeating the same mode or changing only the boundary retains the policy. While the boundary is zero, the private path is suspended.

Finite Off, rest(), tail completion, gaps, release, death and removal take priority. When no Tone lives, Connect allows the next legal onset, including the initial onset and return from a rest. The policy survives individual notes and rests; it does not decide whether each note should sustain or release.

Extending opportunities without a new pitch destination

tie(p) sets the probability of extending a living Tone instead of re-attacking at a selected participation opportunity without a pending target. It is an initial composer parameter for free-pitch sustain() with entrained() or flow() and explicit gate_snap or rearticulate, including a zero boundary. The default 0 preserves current attacks, 1 always extends eligible Tones, and intermediate values mix the two per opportunity. These values are composer choices, not a musical optimum.

A pending target still jumps and attacks. Small adoptions below a positive boundary already glide without pending pitch, so those gliding Tones can extend too: the condition is not exact pitch equality. GateSnap makes small and even zero-distance adoptions pending, so they still attack. Tie reduces attacks to a thinned sequence of existing opportunities; it neither moves the opportunities nor changes pitch proposals, costs or skip. It is not an overall legato amount, a phrase switch, or a response to heard phrase boundaries.

let voices = place(harmonic().sustain().entrained()
    .pitch_apply_mode("rearticulate").rearticulate_above(50).tie(0.5),
    line(220.0, 330.0).count(2));
wait(3.0);
release(voices);

The boundary and intermediate probability in this example illustrate composition, not recommended settings. Extension creates no new Tone, onset metabolism or onset-context learning; the opportunity is consumed, without cost-skip learning or later replay. With articulation_persistence, Connect withholds first without sampling tie. In Base, pending targets attack; only pending-free opportunities sample tie. Tie does not change Base/Connect proposals or retention. Connect’s sliding of large moves and withholding of attacks is a separate policy.

Initial onsets and legal onsets after finite Off, tail completion and rest() are never blocked when no Tone lives. Release, death, removal and rest legality take priority. The parameter cannot change during performance. Switching to Glide suspends it; returning to an applicable mode resumes it. Builder order is independent at place and create; replacements inherit the template value with fresh random and performance state. Finite values clamp to [0,1], positive infinity becomes 1, negative infinity and NaN become 0; unsupported final forms warn and ignore the value. Zero has no tie state or draws, one has no draws, and intermediate values use a separate individual stream at execution only.

Explicit when/duration (Tier 2)

Below the presets sit explicit controls: once(), pulse(rate_hz), while_alive(), cycles(n), and adaptive_duration() (with duration_range, duration_curve, and shorten_on_drop for tuning).

Timing as survival (Tier 3)

Use rhythm_coupling_vitality(lambda_v, v_floor) and rhythm_reward(rho_t, "attack_phase_match") when timing should affect survival and reorganization, and rhythm_freq(freq_hz) to set the internal articulation oscillator directly. These are legacy articulation controls: Gated phonation disables autonomous attacks and applies ordinary consonance recharge to emitted onsets, without the phase-match reward:

let pulse_voice = harmonic()
    .repeat()
    .pulse(2.0)
    .cycles(2)
    .rhythm_freq(2.0)
    .rhythm_coupling_vitality(0.8, 0.4)
    .rhythm_reward(0.4, "attack_phase_match");

place(pulse_voice, at(165.0));
wait(8.0);

Scaffolds (research controls)

The scaffold functions impose an external pulse for comparison assays:

set_scaffold_off();
set_scaffold_shared(2.0);
set_scaffold_scrambled(2.0, 17);

They are useful for demos and assays. They are not the rhythm-composition abstraction: use them only when explicit synchronization is essential to the demo or assay. A dedicated beat carrier is not required for entrained/flow participation.
